01 · What it is

Slack-shaped, but inside your stack.

Raven is a team chat application built on the Frappe Framework. The shape is familiar — public and private channels grouped into workspaces, direct messages, threaded side-discussions, mentions, reactions, file sharing and search. If your team has used Slack or Teams, nobody needs training.

What makes it worth a leaf is where it runs. Raven installs onto an existing Frappe site, which means messages live in your own database on infrastructure you chose, and there is no per-user licence meter running. It sits alongside ERPNext, Frappe HR, CRM and Helpdesk on the same site, and the project’s stated design goal is that any document from any Frappe app can become part of a conversation.

That last point is the whole argument. Generic chat tools integrate with your business systems through webhooks and bots that push notifications about records. Raven is on the same database as the records, so the integration goes the other way too — you can act on a document from inside the chat.

The honest framing

Raven is not trying to out-feature Slack. It is trying to be the chat app you run when your operational system of record is already Frappe, and you would rather have one site, one permissions model and one database than two products and an integration to maintain. Read it as a stack-consolidation play, not a Slack-killer.

03 · The Frappe integration

Where it stops being just chat.

These are the capabilities you cannot get by bolting a chat tool onto a Frappe site from outside, because they depend on being on the same database with the same permissions model.

Capability What it does
Share a documentPaste a link to any document on the site and Raven renders a card showing its status and key fields.
Document notificationsPost to a channel when a document is created, changed or submitted. Conditions and the message template are configured, not coded.
Approve from the chatApprove, reject or send back a document without leaving the conversation.
Who is on leaveLeave records from Frappe HR show next to a person’s name.
Bots, webhooks, agentsBots that post and reply, incoming webhooks that post into channels, and AI agents backed by your own OpenAI or compatible provider.

Read the AI-agent line carefully

Raven’s agents run against your provider credentials, not a Frappe-hosted model. That is the right architecture — you keep control — but it means the model cost, the key management and the cross-border data-processing question are all yours to answer. If your POPIA position is that message content stays in country, pointing an agent at a US inference endpoint reopens exactly the door that self-hosting closed. See where LLM inference runs in SA before you switch agents on.

04 · Versions and install

What it runs on.

Raven branch Frappe branch Stability
mainversion-15 (15.111 or later), version-16Stable
developversion-15 (15.111 or later), version-16, developDevelopment

Two routes to production. On Frappe Cloud, Raven installs onto a site from the marketplace and hosting, upgrades and backups are handled for you. Self-hosted, the project now points at Pilot as the recommended path — it installs Frappe, manages sites and backups, and configures nginx and TLS for production. If you already run a bench, Raven installs the ordinary way.

The version floor is specific

Note 15.111 or later, not just “v15”. A long-running v15 site below that patch level needs updating before Raven will install. And Pilot — the recommended self-host tool — requires Frappe v16 or later, so on a v15 site you are on the manual bench route regardless. Neither is a blocker; both are the kind of thing better discovered now than mid-migration.

05 · Gotchas

Where it gets tricky.

AGPL v3 is network copyleft

Raven is AGPLv3. Running it internally carries no obligation. Offering a modified Raven to third parties over a network does — the licence reaches service provision, not just distribution. Worth noting that this differs across the Frappe estate: Frappe Wiki is MIT. If you plan to fork and resell, get the licence read before the code.

It assumes you are already on Frappe

Every advantage in section 03 comes from sharing a site with your operational data. Standing up a Frappe site purely to run chat gives you a competent Slack alternative and none of the reason to choose it. If Frappe is not already in your stack, this is the wrong entry point.

Chat is a retention and discovery surface

Once approvals happen in channels, the chat log becomes part of your audit trail. Decide early who can export it, how long it is kept, and what happens on an access request — the same questions you would ask of any messaging platform, but now the answers are yours to implement rather than a vendor’s to supply.

Migrating history is its own project

Moving a team off Slack or Teams means deciding what happens to years of existing threads. Treat the import question as a separate piece of work with its own scope, and do not let it hold the rollout hostage.

06 · Decision guide

Use it for, skip it for.

Use it when

  • You already run ERPNext, Frappe HR, CRM or Helpdesk and want chat on the same site
  • Approvals and document context belong in the conversation, not in a notification that links away
  • Per-user Slack or Teams licensing in USD is a line item you want to delete
  • POPIA or a client contract wants message content in a database you control, in a region you chose
  • You want one permissions model across chat and operational data

Skip it when

  • Frappe is not already in your stack — the integration is the only reason to pick this
  • You depend on the Slack or Teams app ecosystem and the integrations your team already relies on
  • Your organisation is paying for Microsoft 365 anyway and Teams is effectively free at the margin
  • You need compliance tooling — retention policies, legal hold, eDiscovery export — that you are not prepared to verify or build yourself
  • Nobody on your team wants to own a self-hosted messaging service, including its uptime
07 · South African context

What this changes for SA delivery.

POPIA — the cleanest residency story in the chat market

Most messaging platforms give you a processing agreement and a region selector. Raven gives you the database. For an SA organisation whose POPIA position is that internal communications stay in country, self-hosting on local infrastructure removes the cross-border transfer question rather than documenting it. That is a materially stronger position than a Section 72 consent flow, and it is the main reason this app is worth considering outside the Frappe faithful.

FX — the arithmetic is simple and it favours self-hosting at scale

Per-seat chat is billed in dollars and scales linearly with headcount. A self-hosted app on a VM you already run does not. The crossover depends on team size and on what your time is worth, but the shape is clear: small teams should not bother, and organisations of a few hundred seats watching the rand should do the sum. Factor in that you are trading a predictable USD subscription for your own operational burden — that is a real cost, just not one that moves with the exchange rate.

The agent caveat, again

If you enable Raven’s AI agents against a US-hosted model provider, message content crosses the border on every call — and you have just reintroduced the exposure that self-hosting removed. Either keep agents off on channels carrying personal information, or run them against local or self-hosted inference. Do not let the self-hosted-chat story paper over a cross-border inference path.
