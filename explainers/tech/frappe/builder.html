Drag a page onto a canvas,

Frappe Builder is a low-code website builder with a Figma-like visual editor, one-click publishing, and — this is the part that separates it from every other page builder — direct access to the Frappe database underneath. Pages can be data-driven against real doctypes without a headless CMS or a separate backend. It ships an AI site-building agent with permission-respecting reads, approval gates on sensitive changes, and revertable edits. MIT licensed, and frappe.io itself runs on it.

01 · What it is

The page builder that can see your data.

Most page builders solve layout and stop. You get a canvas, some blocks, and a publish button, and the moment you need a page driven by real records — a product catalogue, a branch locator, a careers list that reflects open positions — you are wiring a headless CMS to a frontend framework and the "no-code" promise quietly expires.

Frappe Builder's premise is that if your business data already lives in Frappe doctypes, the builder should be able to read them. It uses Frappe Framework's built-in CMS to manage structured content, pull dynamic data from the database, and power data-driven pages with no separate backend. A block's property or style can be bound to a value sourced from a script, from page data, or from a query parameter. Dynamic detail pages — one route pattern, many records — are a first-class thing rather than a workaround.

Around that sits the expected surface: an intuitive visual editor, responsive views for different device widths, dark mode with system-preference detection and a manual override, one-click publishing, and a built-in analytics dashboard covering page views, unique visitors, and top referrers. The maintainers' stated priority from day one was performance — not bloating pages with unnecessary scripts and styles — and the proof point they offer is that frappe.io is itself built on Builder.

MIT, not AGPL

Frappe Builder is MIT licensed, which puts it in a different category from most of the ecosystem around it — Raven, Drive, LMS and Print Designer are all AGPL v3. If you are building something you intend to embed, fork, or ship inside a commercial product, that distinction is the whole conversation. Check the licence file per app; do not infer it from the organisation.

02 · The scripting surface

Where "low-code" becomes code.

Builder is honest about being low-code rather than no-code. There are four distinct places to put logic, and knowing which is which is most of the learning curve.

SurfaceScopeReach for it when
Global scriptsEvery page on the siteAnalytics tags, a consent banner, anything that must be everywhere. Also the easiest place to slow the whole site down.
Client scriptsA single pagePage-specific interactivity that is not worth a component.
Per-block data scriptsOne blockFetching or shaping the data a specific section renders. The workhorse for data-driven sections.
Typed block propsA reusable blockTurning a hand-built section into something a non-technical editor can safely configure. This is the one that decides whether your site is maintainable in a year.

The maintainability trap, said plainly

Every property and style can be bound to a dynamic value from a script, page data, or a query parameter. That is powerful and it is exactly how a "marketing can edit this themselves" site becomes a site only one developer understands. Decide early which parts are editor-facing — typed props, structured content — and which are developer-facing, and resist binding a colour to a script because you can.

03 · The AI site builder

Grounded, gated, revertable — the interesting part isn't the model.

Builder ships an agent, Bob, that builds and edits pages through conversation. The feature list is worth reading closely, because the design decisions in it are more transferable than the feature itself.

By the maintainers' description, Bob grounds itself in your site rather than guessing: it reads existing pages and follows their design language by default, reuses your components and theme tokens, fits new pages into your route structure including dynamic detail pages, wires forms and data-driven sections to real doctypes, and researches facts online instead of inventing them. Reads respect your permissions. Sensitive changes wait for your approval. Every AI edit is revertable.

Those last three clauses are the ones to steal. Most "AI builds your page" features are a text-to-HTML call with no relationship to the system they are editing, which is why the output looks generic and why nobody trusts it near production. Grounding the agent in the existing design tokens and route structure is what makes the output fit; running its reads through the existing permission model is what makes it safe to point at a live site; and revertability is what makes a wrong answer cheap. Whether Bob is good depends on the model behind it and on how disciplined your existing pages are — an agent that follows your design language will faithfully follow a bad one. But the harness design is correct, and it is a reasonable template for any agent you build against your own stack.

The honest caveat: an agent that reads your site and researches online is making outbound calls and sending your page content somewhere. Before pointing it at a client site, know which model provider is in the path and what leaves the building.

04 · Gotchas

Where it gets tricky.

Your marketing site now shares a blast radius with your ERP

Builder pages are served from a Frappe bench. If that bench is the one running ERPNext, a launch-day traffic spike on the homepage and month-end close are contending for the same resources, and a public-facing surface is now attached to your system of record. Either give the website its own bench, or be deliberate about sizing, caching and the edge in front of it.

Data-driven pages are database queries on a public route

Binding a section to a doctype is convenient, and it means an anonymous visitor's page view runs a query against your business database. Check what the Guest role can read, cache aggressively, and think about what an enumerable query parameter exposes before you ship a dynamic detail route.

It is a Frappe site, so SEO and edge behaviour are yours to own

Builder produces lean, Lighthouse-friendly pages — that is a stated design goal and it is real. What you still own is everything a marketing team will ask for: redirects, canonical URLs, sitemaps, structured data, and a CDN in front. None of that is automatic.

Visual builders and version control do not naturally agree

Pages live in the database, not in your repository. That means no pull-request review of a page change, no branch-per-campaign, and staging-to-production promotion is a data-migration question rather than a deploy. Frappe Cloud and bench tooling give you answers; you have to pick one deliberately rather than assuming your usual git workflow applies.

Built-in analytics is not a replacement for analytics

Page views, unique visitors, and top referrers are enough to answer "is anyone reading this". They are not enough to answer "which campaign converted". If attribution matters, you are still adding a proper analytics tool — and then you are back to a consent banner and a POPIA question about it.

05 · Decision guide

Use it for, skip it for.

Use Frappe Builder when

  • Your site needs to render live business data that already lives in Frappe doctypes — catalogue, locations, listings, availability.
  • You want customer-facing portal pages on the same site as the records behind them, under the same permissions.
  • You are already on a bench and adding a website should not mean adding a stack.
  • You want a visual editor a designer can work in, with a typed-props escape hatch for the parts that must stay safe.
  • MIT licensing matters because you intend to fork, embed, or ship it.

Skip Frappe Builder when

  • The site is pure marketing with no relationship to your operational data. Astro, Next.js or a static site on the edge is faster to build and cheaper to run.
  • You need the plugin ecosystem — WordPress has thirty thousand of them and you want six.
  • Your content team needs editorial workflow: drafts, scheduled publishing, multi-stage approval, granular per-field permissions. That is a CMS feature set Builder does not target.
  • Your organisation requires page changes to go through code review. Database-resident pages fight that workflow rather than fitting it.
  • You do not run Frappe and have no plans to. Standing up a bench to get a page builder is the wrong trade.
  • You need multi-language content management as a product feature rather than as duplicated routes.
06 · South African context

Where Builder fits SA delivery.

Latency is the decision nobody makes until launch week

A Frappe bench in Johannesburg or Cape Town serves local visitors well and international visitors poorly. A bench in Europe does the reverse. Builder produces lean pages, which helps, but it cannot fix a 180ms round trip. If the site matters commercially outside SA, put a CDN in front and cache the static parts hard — and remember that the data-driven sections are the ones that will not cache, so design which parts of the page are dynamic with that in mind.

POPIA · forms are collection points

Builder wires forms to real doctypes, which means a contact form on a public page writes personal information straight into your operational database with no integration step. That is the convenience and the obligation in one sentence. Each form needs a stated purpose, a lawful basis, a retention rule, and a consent mechanism that matches what you actually do with the submission. The ease of wiring it up is precisely why this gets skipped.

FX · the quiet win

A website built on a bench you already run adds no new USD subscription. No page-builder SaaS, no headless CMS seat, no separate hosting plan. For an SA studio running client sites, that is a margin question as much as a technical one — the recurring cost stays in rand and stays yours. Weigh it against the maintenance you are taking on: one more surface to patch, back up, and keep up with upstream.
