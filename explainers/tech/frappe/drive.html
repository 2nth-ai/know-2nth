tech/frappe · Drive · Skill Leaf

File storage that stays
on your side of the border.

Frappe Drive is an open-source file storage, sharing, and collaboration platform — a file manager with previews, streaming and granular sharing, plus Writer, a real-time collaborative document editor built on Yjs. It is the self-hosted answer to Google Drive for organisations that need the files to live somewhere specific. It is also, by its maintainers' own README, still in beta — and that single fact should shape how you use it.

Live Frappe Framework app AGPL v3 Beta — per upstream
01 · What it is

Two products wearing one app.

Frappe Drive is really two things shipped together, and it helps to evaluate them separately.

Core is the file manager: multi-part uploads for large files, folder uploads that preserve structure, in-browser previews across a documented set of file types, video streamed straight from the server, search across your files and files shared with you, and per-file activity logs covering permission and metadata changes. Sharing is granular — individual users, groups, everyone on the site, or published publicly — and guest users can be given deliberately limited access. Storage is either pooled across all users or quota'd per user.

Writer is the collaborative document editor: real-time co-editing with users and guests, annotations you can resolve and reply to, manual versioning alongside automatic versioning, and .docx import. It is a genuine collaborative editor, not a textarea with a save button.

The origin story is worth a line, because it explains the shape. Frappe has had robust file handling in the framework since forever; the idea of a standalone Drive has been floating since at least 2015. What started as file sharing turned into a collaboration tool on the way — which is why Writer exists at all, and why the two halves feel like different products.

Read the beta warning as written

The upstream README opens with a warning: Frappe Drive is in beta, and taking backups in production use is strongly advised. That is the maintainers being straight with you, and it deserves to be repeated rather than buried. It does not mean "do not use it". It means: do not make it the only copy of anything, do not put the finance team's only set of signed contracts in it, and do run the backup you were going to run anyway.

The dependency list tells you the operational story.

Drive's Python manifest is unusually informative. Each of these dependencies is an operational commitment you are taking on when you self-host.

The bit people miss: your app server becomes a media server

"Stream videos directly from the server" is a headline feature and an infrastructure decision. Video streaming is long-lived, bandwidth-heavy, and completely unlike ERP request traffic. If your team puts site walkthroughs or training recordings in Drive, the same box running month-end close is now serving them. Put Drive on its own bench, front it with a CDN, or push the objects to S3-compatible storage and serve from there — but decide, rather than finding out during a demo.

03 · Gotchas

Where it gets tricky.

Beta means the migration path is not guaranteed

Pre-1.0 software reserves the right to change its data model. Backups are the stated mitigation, and they are necessary, but the deeper point is that an export path matters more than a backup: you want to be able to get every file out as files, not just restore a database. Test that before you migrate a department onto it.

Preview coverage is a fixed list, not "everything"

Supported file previews are documented upstream, and CAD, large PSDs, and specialist formats generally are not on it. Files still store and download fine — they just do not preview. If your users' mental model is "Drive shows me my files", check the list against what your organisation actually stores before rollout.

docx import is import, not round-trip

Writer imports .docx into its own editor. That is a one-way conversion, and complex Word documents — tracked changes, sophisticated styles, embedded objects — will not survive intact. It is a migration tool, not interoperability. Teams that must exchange Word files with outside parties should keep doing that outside Drive.

Storage quotas are a policy you have to choose

Drive supports pooling storage across all users or assigning a per-user quota. Neither is the "obviously right" default: pooling is friendlier until one person uploads 400GB of drone footage, and per-user quotas are predictable until someone legitimately needs more. Pick deliberately, and put a monitor on the volume either way.

Guest access is a real permission surface

Guest users with limited, controlled access to your site are genuinely useful for client collaboration — and they are accounts on the same site as your ERP. Audit what the guest role can reach across every installed app, not just Drive. "Limited in Drive" is not the same claim as "limited on the bench".

04 · Decision guide

Use it for, skip it for.

Use Frappe Drive when

  • Data residency is the requirement and feature parity with Google Drive is not — this is the whole reason the app exists.
  • You already run a Frappe bench and want document storage under the same users, roles, and audit trail.
  • You need a collaborative editor that runs on infrastructure you control, and Writer's feature set is enough.
  • You want to give clients or contractors scoped guest access to a folder without buying them seats somewhere.
  • You are piloting, with backups, and you can live with a pre-1.0 data model.

Skip Frappe Drive when

  • It would be the system of record for anything you cannot afford to lose. Beta is the maintainers' word, not ours.
  • You need offline desktop sync — a Drive or Dropbox client that keeps a local folder in step. There is no equivalent here.
  • Your users live in Office and expect real .docx round-tripping, co-authoring in Word, and Excel.
  • You need retention policies, legal hold, eDiscovery, or DLP as product features rather than as things you build.
  • You want mobile apps. Drive is a web application.
  • Residency is genuinely satisfiable by a hyperscaler region you already trust — in which case managed object storage plus an existing suite is less work and more reliable.
05 · South African context

The residency case, honestly stated.

POPIA · this is the strongest argument for the app

Document stores are where personal information accumulates without anyone deciding it should: ID copies, medical certificates, signed contracts, CVs, bank confirmations. Section 72 governs transferring that across the border, and the usual answer for a SaaS suite is a contractual one. Self-hosting Drive in an SA region (af-south-1, africa-south1, southafricanorth) makes it a factual one instead: the objects are in-country, the access log is yours, and the operator is you. For regulated clients that ask "where exactly is this file", that is a materially easier conversation.

Where that argument stops

Residency is not security, and self-hosting moves the burden rather than removing it. You now own patching, backup verification, ransomware resilience, and the access review you used to outsource. An organisation that cannot staff that is safer on a hyperscaler with a local region than on a beta app on an unpatched box. Be honest with the client about which of those two you are actually selling them.

FX and egress

Per-seat cloud storage is USD-billed and scales with headcount; self-hosted storage is rand-billed and scales with terabytes. Which is cheaper depends entirely on which of those two grows faster in your organisation. Do include egress: SA bandwidth is not free, and a Drive full of video that staff stream from home is a data-transfer line item, not just a disk one. Object storage plus a CDN in front is usually the cheaper shape once the library gets large.
