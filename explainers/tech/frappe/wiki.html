tech/frappe · Wiki · Skill Leaf

Documentation with
an approval queue.

Frappe Wiki is a markdown-first knowledge base built on the Frappe Framework — pages, revision history, a change-approval workflow, attachments, auto-generated tables of contents, and Redis-backed search. Its two distinguishing traits are that edits can require review before they publish, and that publishing a page or a fix happens live with no rebuild and no downtime. It is MIT-licensed, which in this suite is the exception rather than the rule.

01 · What it is

A wiki Frappe built because nothing else fit.

Frappe Wiki exists for an unglamorous reason: Frappe needed somewhere to write the ERPNext documentation, looked at the available open-source options, and did not like any of them enough to adopt one. So they wrote their own, and then kept using it for user manuals, company policies and general internal knowledge. That origin shows in the product — it is shaped around long-form, text-heavy, reviewed content rather than around freeform team note-taking.

The feature set is deliberately narrow. Pages are authored in markdown. Every page carries revision history. Edits can be routed through a change-approval workflow, so a contributor's change waits for a reviewer before it goes live. Pages take attachments. A table of contents is generated automatically from the heading structure. And behaviour can be extended with custom scripts configured in Wiki Settings, which is the standard Frappe escape hatch rather than a plugin marketplace.

Under the hood it is Frappe Framework, the Ace editor for the markdown editing surface, and RediSearch for indexing and search. RediSearch is the notable one: search is a real inverted index in Redis, not a LIKE query against a text column, which is why it stays usable as the page count grows.

MIT, in a suite where that is unusual

Frappe Wiki is MIT-licensed. So is Frappe Builder, and so is Frappe Framework itself. Almost everything else is not: ERPNext and Frappe HR are GPLv3; Helpdesk, CRM, Books, Drive, Learning and Print Designer are AGPLv3; Raven is AGPLv3 under a third-party maintainer. If you need to embed a knowledge base into a product you ship to customers, Wiki's licence is the one in this suite that does not put a network-copyleft obligation in your path. That is a real and underappreciated reason to reach for it.

02 · The approval workflow

The feature that decides whether you want this.

Most wikis optimise for frictionless contribution: anyone edits, anyone publishes, the history sorts out disputes afterwards. Frappe Wiki can work that way, but it also supports the opposite — a contributor proposes, a reviewer approves, and only then does the page change for readers.

You want Frappe Wiki fits? Why
Reviewed public documentationStrong fitThe approval queue is the point. Contributions from outside the core team land as proposals, not as live edits.
Policies and controlled proceduresStrong fitRevision history plus review gives you an auditable answer to "who changed this and who signed it off".
Product or API documentationGood fitMarkdown source, live publishing without a rebuild step, and search that scales.
Fast internal team notesWeak fitReview friction is wrong for a scratchpad. The team will stop using it and go back to chat.
Rich collaborative documentsWrong toolNo real-time co-editing, no comment threads on selections. That is Frappe Drive's Writer, not this.
A structured course or curriculumWrong toolNo enrolment, progress tracking or assessment. That is Frappe Learning.

Publishing without downtime is the underrated half

Because pages are database records rendered on request, a typo fix or a whole new page goes live the moment it is approved. There is no static-site build, no CI pipeline, no deploy. Set against a docs-as-code stack — markdown in git, built by a generator, deployed on merge — you lose the reviewer's familiarity with pull requests and gain the ability to let a non-technical subject-matter expert correct their own page without touching a repository. Which of those you want is a real decision, and it mostly turns on who your authors are.

03 · How it works

Install, and read it from an agent.

Pages are doctypes. That makes the wiki readable over the standard REST API, which is the property that matters if you intend to ground an assistant in it.

# Install on an existing bench
bench get-app https://github.com/frappe/wiki
bench --site sitename install-app wiki
# Pull published pages for indexing into a retrieval pipeline
GET /api/resource/Wiki Page
  ?filters=[["published","=",1]]
  &fields=["name","title","route","content","modified"]
  &order_by=modified desc
  &limit_page_length=0

# Because content is markdown, it needs no HTML-to-text conversion
# before chunking — which is one less lossy step than scraping
# a rendered docs site.

A wiki an agent can actually use

Markdown source over a REST API, with a modified timestamp on every page, is close to the ideal shape for grounding an assistant. You can pull only what changed since the last run, chunk clean markdown rather than stripped HTML, and rely on the same permission model the human readers are subject to. If part of why you are standing up a knowledge base is so that an internal agent can answer from it, this is a materially better starting point than a rendered documentation site you would otherwise have to scrape.

04 · Gotchas

Where it gets tricky.

Search depends on RediSearch actually being present

Frappe Wiki indexes through RediSearch. If the Redis instance backing the site does not have the module loaded — easy to hit on a hand-rolled deployment or a managed Redis that does not offer modules — search degrades or fails while the rest of the wiki works perfectly. Verify search on a populated site, not an empty one, because an empty wiki returns nothing either way.

The approval workflow needs owners, not just configuration

Turning on review is easy. Sustaining it is a staffing question. An approval queue with no committed reviewer becomes a backlog, contributors watch their edits sit unpublished, and within a month people stop contributing. If nobody owns the queue, do not turn it on — an unreviewed wiki that is current beats a reviewed one that is stale.

Two docs URLs exist and they are not the same

The project's own README links to docs.frappe.io/wiki-v2/ in its header and docs.frappe.io/wiki/ further down. They correspond to different versions. Check which one matches the app version you have installed before following a setup guide, and treat the repository itself as the authority when they disagree.

Markdown means markdown

The editing surface is the Ace code editor, not a WYSIWYG canvas. For engineers this is a feature. For the operations manager you need to own the standard operating procedures, it is a barrier — and the person who will not learn markdown syntax is the person whose knowledge you most needed to capture. Pilot with your actual intended authors before committing the organisation.

Custom scripts are power and rope in equal measure

Wiki Settings lets you extend behaviour with custom scripts. That is the standard Frappe extensibility story, and it means wiki behaviour can end up defined in a settings field that no code review ever sees and no backup obviously captures as code. Keep anything non-trivial in a versioned custom app instead.

05 · Decision guide

Use it for, skip it for.

Use Frappe Wiki when

  • Documentation accuracy matters enough that you want changes reviewed before readers see them.
  • You are already on Frappe and want the knowledge base under the same users, roles and API as everything else.
  • Your authors are comfortable in markdown — engineers, technical writers, product people.
  • You want an internal assistant grounded in your documentation, and would rather query clean markdown over REST than scrape a rendered site.
  • Licence matters. MIT is the loosest licence in this suite, and the only one here that will not complicate a product you ship.
  • Publishing needs to be immediate — a correction goes live when approved, with no build or deploy in between.

Skip Frappe Wiki when

  • You need real-time collaborative editing or comment threads. Reach for Frappe Drive's Writer, or a purpose-built docs tool.
  • Your authors will not write markdown. This is the most common reason internal wikis die, and no amount of enthusiasm at kickoff fixes it.
  • You want a general-purpose team workspace — databases, boards, embeds, freeform pages. This is a documentation tool with a narrow remit, by design.
  • Your team already works docs-as-code in git and is happy there. Pull-request review is a stronger workflow for engineers than an in-app approval queue.
  • You are not otherwise on Frappe. Standing up a bench for a wiki alone is disproportionate; the integration is most of the value.
  • You need a rich page-permission model. Frappe roles are capable but this is not a per-page-ACL product.
06 · South African context

Where this lands in SA delivery.

Reviewed documentation is an audit artefact

For organisations carrying real compliance load — financial services under the FSCA, anything touching the FIC Act, ISO-certified operations — "we document our procedures" is weaker than "our procedures are versioned, and each change has a named approver and a timestamp". Frappe Wiki's revision history plus approval queue produces that evidence as a by-product of ordinary work, rather than as a separate compliance exercise nobody maintains. That is worth more than the feature list suggests.

POPIA · the wiki is where operational knowledge leaks

Two habits to design against. First, examples: a page explaining how to process a claim gets written using a real claim, with a real name and a real ID number, and that personal information now lives permanently in the revision history where nobody thinks to look for it. Use synthetic data in documentation, and say so in the contribution guidance. Second, retention: revision history is by nature append-only, so anything published once persists. If a page must be scrubbed, understand that removing the current version does not remove the history.

Institutional memory in a market with real mobility

Skilled technical people move — between employers, and offshore to remote roles paid in hard currency. The organisations that survive that well are the ones where the knowledge is written down rather than resident in whoever has been there longest. A wiki is the cheapest insurance available against that, and the review workflow is what stops it decaying into a graveyard of half-true pages. The tool is not the hard part; the practice is. Budget for someone to own it.
