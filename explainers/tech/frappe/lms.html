tech/frappe · Learning · Skill Leaf

Training records that
join to the employee.

Frappe Learning is an open-source LMS built as a Frappe app — courses, chapters, lessons, quizzes, assignments, batches, live classes, programming exercises and certificates, all as doctypes. The feature set is respectable; the reason to choose it is that completion data lives next to your HR and ERP records instead of behind someone else's export button. Where it falls short for South African buyers, it falls short clearly — see section 06.

Live Frappe Framework app AGPL v3 Vue · Frappe UI v16.4.0 · Nov 2026 SCORM import
01 · What it is

Courses as documents, not a black box.

Frappe Learning is the LMS the Frappe team built because Moodle's course authoring was, in their words, long forms and a confusing interface. It started as the platform behind Mon.School for FOSS United and grew into a standalone app: courses split into chapters, chapters into lessons, learners grouped into batches, quizzes and assignments attached to lessons, and certificates at the end.

What distinguishes it from the LMS field is not the feature list — it is that every one of those things is an ordinary Frappe doctype. A course is a document. An enrolment is a document with a progress field. A quiz submission is a document. That means course data joins to the rest of your site: to Employee in Frappe HR, to Customer in ERPNext, to a Raven channel for the cohort. Reporting on "which of our technicians have completed the compliance module" is a query, not an export and a spreadsheet.

It is more capable than the "lightweight alternative" framing suggests. Quizzes support single-choice, multiple-choice and open-ended questions with max_attempts, passing_percentage, shuffle_questions and a violation log. Chapters can import SCORM packages. There are programming exercises with starter code and test cases, assignment submissions as PDF or document, Zoom and Google Meet live classes against batches, paid batches with coupons, and an evaluator workflow where a human scores a certification request before the certificate issues.

Where this fits the agent story

Training content is the most commonly cited reason organisations say they cannot let agents touch a process: nobody can prove who was trained on what. With the LMS on the same site as the ERP and HR data, that proof is a joined query rather than an attestation. The same REST surface that serves the course catalogue serves "list employees whose certificate for this course expires in the next 60 days" — LMS Certificate carries an expiry_date, so recertification is a scheduled report, not a diarised reminder someone owns personally.

03 · How it works

REST against the course catalogue.

Enrolments, progress and certificates are documents, so the integration surface is the standard Frappe API. The useful calls are reporting calls.

# Published courses, for a catalogue page or an agent's context
GET /api/resource/LMS Course
  ?filters=[["published","=",1]]
  &fields=["name","title","short_introduction","enable_certification"]
  &order_by=title asc
# Who has not finished the compliance module?
GET /api/resource/LMS Enrollment
  ?filters=[["course","=","popia-awareness"],["progress","<",100]]
  &fields=["member","member_name","progress","current_lesson"]
  &order_by=progress asc
  &limit_page_length=500

# Certificates expiring in the next 60 days — the recertification queue
GET /api/resource/LMS Certificate
  ?filters=[["expiry_date","between",["2026-11-23","2027-01-22"]]]
  &fields=["member_name","course_title","issue_date","expiry_date"]
# Enrol someone programmatically — e.g. on an HR onboarding hook
POST /api/resource/LMS Enrollment
{
  "member": "thandi@example.co.za",
  "course": "popia-awareness"
}

The hook is where this gets useful. Frappe's hook system means you don't poll for any of this. An after_insert on Employee can create the induction enrolments; a hook on LMS Quiz Submission can write the result back to a competency field on the employee record or post it into a Raven channel. The LMS stops being a destination people must remember to visit and becomes a step in a workflow that already runs.

04 · Gotchas

Where it gets tricky.

No SETA, QCTO or B-BBEE reporting

This is the big one for South African buyers and it needs saying plainly: the app ships no SA statutory training reporting. No Workplace Skills Plan or Annual Training Report output, no SETA learner upload format, no QCTO alignment, no B-BBEE skills-development evidence pack. The data to produce those things is all there and queryable; the reports are build work. Scope them as a project, the same way the Frappe HR leaf scopes EMP201 and IRP5.

SCORM in, nothing out

Chapters can import a SCORM package, which covers reusing vendor-supplied compliance content. There is no SCORM or xAPI export, and no LRS integration. Courses you author here live here. If your training content has to be portable to a client's LMS, author it somewhere else.

Live classes depend on a third party

Zoom and Google Meet live classes are integrations with their own settings doctypes and their own credentials. The LMS schedules and links the session; it does not host video. Those are separate subscriptions, usually dollar-priced, and they are not optional if synchronous delivery is your model.

Proctoring is a violation log, not invigilation

There is an LMS Quiz Violation Log and quiz settings for attempt limits and question shuffling. That is deterrence and an audit trail. It is not identity verification or screen monitoring. For an assessment that carries real consequence, either invigilate in person or buy a proctoring service.

Paid courses mean you are now in payments

Batches can be paid, with coupons and payment records. That pulls a payment gateway, refunds, invoicing and tax treatment into scope. If you only need internal training, leave the paid-batch machinery switched off rather than half-configured.

05 · Decision guide

Use it for, skip it for.

Use Frappe Learning when

  • You are already on Frappe and want training records joined to employees, customers or partners rather than sitting in a separate system with its own user list.
  • Internal enablement, partner certification or customer onboarding is the job — moderate catalogues, cohorts you control, outcomes you report on.
  • You want training completion to be queryable evidence in a compliance or audit conversation, with certificate expiry driving recertification automatically.
  • You want to fold existing SCORM compliance content in alongside courses you author.
  • Authoring speed matters more than breadth — this is a genuinely faster course builder than Moodle, which is the reason it exists.

Skip Frappe Learning when

  • You need SETA, QCTO or B-BBEE skills-development reporting out of the box. Nothing here produces it, and pretending otherwise is how a training project misses a scorecard deadline.
  • You are an accredited training provider whose content must export to other systems. No SCORM or xAPI export is a hard stop.
  • You need the Moodle plugin ecosystem, or the integration depth of Docebo, Cornerstone or SAP SuccessFactors Learning. The ecosystem is not comparable.
  • Your delivery is primarily live and synchronous at scale. The video is someone else's product and you will be assembling the experience yourself.
  • You need serious learning analytics — cohort comparison, content effectiveness, predictive drop-off. You get progress fields and quiz results, and you build the rest.
  • You are not otherwise on Frappe. Standalone, the comparison against Moodle or a hosted LMS is much less flattering, because the integration advantage is the whole argument.
06 · South African context

Skills development is a scorecard line, not a nice-to-have.

Training in South Africa carries statutory weight that global LMS comparisons ignore entirely. That cuts both for and against this app, and the honest answer depends on which part you need.

Why the structure fits. Skills development is a weighted element of the B-BBEE scorecard, the Skills Development Levy is payable at 1% of payroll above the R500,000 annual threshold, and SETA grants are recoverable against a submitted Workplace Skills Plan and Annual Training Report. All of that reporting is fundamentally a question of who was trained on what, when, for how long, and at what cost — joined to employee demographics. An LMS whose enrolments and certificates are queryable documents on the same site as Employee records is structurally the right shape for that evidence. A hosted LMS with a CSV export and no link to your HR data is not.

Why you should still budget build work. Structurally right is not the same as done. There is no WSP or ATR output, no SETA upload format, no learnership tracking, no QCTO alignment. You will be writing those reports against the REST API, and you want someone who has submitted a WSP before to specify them — the same advice this tree gives about putting a payroll specialist on EMP501 before a first production run. The honest framing for a client is: the system of record is sound and cheap; the statutory reporting layer is a discrete, scoped project on top of it.

Cost and connectivity. No per-learner licence in dollars is a real saving, and it is the saving that makes training a large casual workforce viable at all — per-seat LMS pricing is what stops most SA employers training people they do not consider permanent. Set against that: video-heavy courses on mobile data are expensive for the learner, not the employer. Lessons that lean on text, images and short clips, with the youtube field used sparingly, will complete at materially higher rates than a course built as an hour of streamed video. Self-hosting in an SA region keeps latency sensible and keeps learner personal information — which under POPIA includes assessment results and anything in a submitted assignment — in the country.
