tech/frappe · Learning · Skill Leaf

Courses, batches,
certificates — on your site.

Frappe Learning is an open-source learning management system on the Frappe Framework. Courses structured as chapters and lessons, learners grouped into batches with scheduled live classes, quizzes and assignments, and certificates on completion. The app is still called lms in the repository and on install; the product is Frappe Learning.

Live Frappe Framework app AGPL v3 Vue frontend
01 · What it is

An LMS built because Moodle annoyed someone.

Frappe Learning has an unusually honest origin story, and it tells you what the product optimises for. In 2021 the team needed a learning management system to launch Mon.School for FOSS United. They evaluated Moodle and, in their own words, it did not feel right — the forms were unnecessarily lengthy and the interface was confusing. So they wrote something simpler, and it became a product.

That is worth taking at face value. This is not an attempt to match Moodle feature for feature. It is an attempt to make creating a course straightforward, on the theory that the reason most internal training content never ships is that the authoring tool made it tedious. If your blocker is course-creation friction rather than assessment sophistication, that is the right trade.

It runs on the Frappe Framework, so learners, instructors and records are doctypes on a site you control, with the same REST API and permissions model as everything else in the ecosystem — including Frappe HR, which is the pairing that makes this interesting for internal training.

The naming is split and it will cost you ten minutes

The product is Frappe Learning. The repository is frappe/lms, the app you install is lms, and the hub card in this tree says LMS. The container image is ghcr.io/frappe/lms. None of this is a problem; it just means searching for documentation under the wrong name returns less than you expect. Search for both.

02 · What you get

The feature surface.

Area What ships
Structured learningA three-level hierarchy: a course contains chapters, and chapters group lessons. The chapter is what sets the context for its lessons.
BatchesGroup learners by course and duration. A batch is the unit that scheduling and cohort management hang off.
Live classesCreate Zoom live classes for a batch from inside the app. Learners see the list of classes their batch is expected to attend.
QuizzesQuestions can be single-choice, multiple-choice, or open-ended.
AssignmentsInstructors set assignments; learners submit as PDFs or documents.
CertificatesGranted on course or batch completion. Use the built-in template or supply your own.

Three levels is a constraint, and usually the right one

Course → chapter → lesson is fixed. You do not get arbitrary nesting. For most internal training, partner enablement and customer academies that is sufficient and it keeps authoring fast. If your curriculum genuinely needs deeper structure — a multi-year qualification with modules, units and nested outcomes — you will be flattening it to fit, and you should check that the flattening survives contact with whoever signs off the curriculum.

03 · Assessment, honestly

What the quiz engine does and does not do for you.

The assessment story needs reading carefully, because “quizzes and assignments” implies more automation than is on offer.

Single-choice and multiple-choice questions mark themselves. Open-ended questions do not. Nor do assignments submitted as PDFs or documents — someone reads those. So the moment your assessment design includes written answers, you have committed a human to marking, and the LMS is the submission and record-keeping layer rather than the grading engine. That is a perfectly sound design; it is just not zero-effort assessment, and it is the single most common misunderstanding when a training manager is shown an LMS demo.

Plan the marking workload as part of the course design, not as an afterthought. A 200-learner cohort with three open-ended questions per module is a staffing decision.

Interoperability: verify, do not assume

If your training function depends on SCORM or xAPI — because you are importing third-party course packages, or exporting completion data into a corporate LMS — confirm support directly against the current documentation before committing. The project does not advertise these standards among its features, and a standards gap discovered after content migration is an expensive surprise. We are flagging this as a question to answer, not asserting an absence.

04 · Gotchas

Where it gets tricky.

Zoom is the named live-class integration

Live classes are created as Zoom classes from inside the app. If your organisation standardised on Microsoft Teams or Google Meet, that is a gap to design around — most teams solve it by scheduling outside the app and linking in, which works but loses the integrated learner view that made the feature attractive. Confirm the current integration list before you promise a Teams workflow.

Open-ended assessment is human work

Covered above, and worth repeating because it is the assumption that breaks project plans. Choice questions self-mark; written answers and document submissions need a marker.

AGPL v3 is network copyleft

Frappe Learning is AGPLv3. Internal use carries no obligation. Offering a modified version as a service to third parties does — which is precisely the shape of “we will white-label this as a client academy”. If that is the plan, get the licence read before the content is built. Frappe Wiki is MIT; the estate is not uniform.

Certificates are documents, not credentials

The app issues certificates from a template. That is presentation, not accreditation — it carries exactly as much weight as your organisation’s reputation gives it. If learners need a credential recognised by a professional body or a SETA, the recognition comes from your accreditation, not from the PDF this generates.

05 · Decision guide

Use it for, skip it for.

Use it when

  • You run structured training — employee onboarding, partner enablement, a customer academy
  • You are already on Frappe and want learner records beside employee records in Frappe HR
  • Course-authoring friction is what has been stopping your training content from shipping
  • Learner personal information and assessment records should stay in a database you control
  • Cohort-based delivery with scheduled live classes fits how you actually teach
  • You want to query training data directly rather than request a report from a vendor

Skip it when

  • You need SCORM or xAPI interoperability and cannot verify it is supported
  • Your curriculum requires deeper structure than course → chapter → lesson without distortion
  • You need proctoring, identity verification or serious anti-cheat for high-stakes assessment
  • Your live-class platform is Teams or Meet and the integrated class view is a requirement rather than a nice-to-have
  • You want a hosted academy with no infrastructure to own — buy a SaaS LMS instead
  • Nobody is available to mark open-ended work, and your assessment design depends on it
06 · South African context

What this changes for SA delivery.

POPIA — learner records are a sharper case than people expect

An LMS accumulates names, ID numbers, employment details, assessment results and sometimes disciplinary or performance context. For SA organisations, and particularly for accredited training providers, that is a meaningful special-personal-information footprint. Running the LMS on your own infrastructure keeps it in country and under your own access rules — which is a materially easier position to defend than a US-hosted platform with a Section 72 transfer and consent flow behind it.

Reporting obligations — the real SA advantage is SQL access

SA training providers carrying SETA or QCTO reporting obligations need learner and completion data in specific shapes, and those shapes change. On a hosted LMS, an unsupported report is a vendor feature request with no committed date. Here the data is in doctypes on your own database, so an awkward report is a query you write this week. Verify the fields you need are captured — but the ceiling on what you can report is your SQL, not a product roadmap.

FX — per-learner pricing is the thing self-hosting kills

Commercial LMS platforms commonly price per active learner per month, in dollars. That model punishes exactly the thing you want — more people doing more training — and it does so at an exchange rate you do not control. Self-hosted, a cohort of 50 and a cohort of 500 cost the same licence: nothing. Infrastructure and your own time are the cost, and both are rand-denominated. For organisations running recurring enablement at scale, this is the clearest cost case in this sub-hub.
