Frappe Learning is an open-source LMS on the Frappe Framework. Courses organised into chapters and lessons, learners grouped into time-boxed batches, live classes via Zoom or Google Meet, and certificates. Two things set it apart: coding exercises marked automatically against hidden test cases, and certification that books a real evaluator rather than reading a completion percentage.

01 · What it is

An LMS that started as a real school.

Simpler than Moodle by design, with a few capabilities Moodle does not have at all.

Frappe Learning is an open-source learning management system built on the Frappe Framework. Courses organised into chapters and lessons, learners grouped into batches, quizzes and assignments, live classes, and certificates at the end.

Its origin explains its shape. In 2021 the Frappe team needed an LMS to launch Mon.School for FOSS United, looked at Moodle, and found the forms too long and the interface confusing. So they built one — and it became a product. That lineage shows: it is noticeably simpler to put a course online here than in Moodle, and correspondingly less configurable when you need something Moodle would let you bend.

Licensed AGPL v3, with the latest tagged release at the time of writing being v2.64.0. The app is mature by release count and genuinely in production — Frappe School runs on it.

02 · The learning model

Three levels, then batches on top.

Content is a three-level hierarchy: a LMS Course contains Course Chapter records, each containing Course Lesson records. The chapter exists to set context for its lessons rather than to carry content itself. Above courses, LMS Program groups several courses into a longer pathway.

Delivery is separate from content, which is the design decision that makes the rest work. A LMS Batch is a cohort of learners taking a course over a defined period, with its own timetable, its own live classes and its own feedback. The same course can run as a dozen batches over two years without duplicating a single lesson.

03 · What it does that Moodle does not

Coding exercises and booked evaluations.

Two capabilities here are unusual enough to be the reason you pick this over a more established LMS.

The first is in-browser programming exercises with automated marking. A LMS Programming Exercise holds a problem statement, starter code and a language; LMS Test Case records hold input and expected output, and critically carry a hidden flag so learners cannot simply code to the visible cases. Submissions run against the full set. For teaching development — which is a large share of what SA skills programmes actually need — this removes the usual choice between manual marking and bolting on a separate platform.

The second is evaluator scheduling for certification. Rather than a certificate falling out of a completion percentage, a learner can request certification, and Course Evaluator plus Evaluator Schedule books them into a live assessment slot with a human. That is a meaningfully more defensible certificate, and the booking machinery is in the box.

Live classes are handled through either Zoom or Google Meet, each with its own settings doctype, created directly from a batch. There is also a learner-profile and jobs layer — User Skill, Work Experience, Education Detail, Job Opportunity and LMS Job Application — which turns the platform into a small talent marketplace. Whether you want that is a different question, but it is there and it is coherent: learners who complete courses have profiles employers can post against.

04 · Gotchas

Where it gets tricky.

Billing is built around Indian tax law

LMS Payment carries gstin, pan and amount_with_gst — Indian GST registration, Indian permanent account number, and GST-inclusive totals. There is no SA VAT equivalent in the payment doctype. If you intend to sell courses in South Africa, treat the built-in payment flow as a starting point you will extend, and plan to raise tax invoices from your accounting system rather than from the LMS.

Proctoring is a log, not an invigilator

LMS Quiz Violation Log records events with a severity, which is useful evidence. It is not proctoring in the sense an accrediting body may mean — no identity verification, no video capture, no lockdown browser. Do not describe an assessment as proctored on the strength of this field.

Accreditation is entirely on you

Nothing in the app makes a course SETA-aligned, NQF-registered or accredited by anyone. It gives you the record-keeping substrate — enrolments, progress, assessments, certificates, evaluator sign-off — and the mapping to a qualification, the moderation, and the reporting format are work you still do. Anyone promising otherwise has not read the doctypes.

Simplicity is a ceiling as well as a feature

The three-level hierarchy is deliberately rigid. No arbitrary nesting, no conditional release rules of real depth, no learning-path branching on assessment outcome. If your pedagogy needs adaptive sequencing, this is the wrong tool and Moodle's complexity is the price of what you want.

No SCORM, no xAPI

There is no support for importing SCORM packages or emitting xAPI statements. Existing bought-in course libraries will not load, and your LMS data will not flow into an LRS. For organisations with an existing content investment this is often the deciding constraint.

Video hosting is your problem

LMS Video Watch Duration tracks progress, but the platform is not a video pipeline — no transcoding, no adaptive bitrate, no CDN. On South African last-mile connections that matters more than it does elsewhere: either host video somewhere that does adaptive streaming, or accept that rural learners will struggle.

05 · Decision guide

Use it for, skip it for.

Use Frappe Learning when

  • You are teaching technical skills and want automated marking of coding exercises without a second platform.
  • You want certificates backed by a booked human evaluation rather than a completion percentage.
  • You already run Frappe and want learners, employees and courses on one site with one permission model.
  • You are running cohort-based training — batches with timetables and live classes — rather than purely self-paced content.
  • Moodle's configurability has become the problem rather than the solution, and you want something a subject expert can actually publish into.

Skip Frappe Learning when

  • You have an existing SCORM content library. It will not import, and there is no xAPI path out either.
  • Your programme needs adaptive learning paths that branch on assessment results.
  • You need accredited-provider features out of the box — moderation workflows, NQF mapping, statutory reporting formats.
  • Selling courses with compliant SA VAT invoicing is central and you do not want to extend the payment doctype.
  • You need serious proctoring for high-stakes assessment.
  • You are not on Frappe and the LMS is the only reason you would be. Moodle and the hosted platforms are a shorter path.
06 · South African context

Where this lands in SA delivery.

South African organisations have a structural reason to care about an LMS that most markets do not. The Skills Development Levy is payable by employers above the payroll threshold, and the skills-development element of B-BBEE scorecards rewards verifiable training spend and learner throughput. Both reward being able to show who was trained, on what, for how long, and with what outcome — which is a record-keeping problem before it is a pedagogy problem.

That is where Frappe Learning is genuinely well suited. Enrolments, progress, video watch duration, assessment submissions, evaluator sign-off and issued certificates are all queryable doctypes on a site you control. If payroll already runs on Frappe HR on the same instance, the join between employee and training record is a link field rather than a reconciliation exercise. Pulling together an audit pack stops being a quarterly scramble.

The caveat is the one worth repeating: none of that makes the training accredited. Alignment to a qualification, moderation, and the specific report formats a SETA or verification agency wants are work the tool does not do for you. What it gives you is clean, exportable evidence — which is the part organisations usually get wrong.

Two practical notes. POPIA applies squarely here: assessment results, and anything in the learner-profile layer, are personal information, and some of it is arguably special personal information once disability accommodations or health-related absence enter the picture. Self-hosting keeps that on-shore, but set retention deliberately — there is no reason to keep a failed quiz attempt from 2021. And on bandwidth: because video hosting is yours to solve, test the learner experience on a constrained mobile connection before you commit to a video-heavy curriculum.
