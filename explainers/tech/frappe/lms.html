tech/frappe · Learning · Skill Leaf

Courses, batches,
and a certificate at the end.

Frappe Learning — still lms in the repository and on the bench — is an open-source learning management system built on the Frappe Framework. Courses structured as chapters and lessons, learners grouped into batches, Zoom live classes scheduled from inside the app, quizzes and assignments, and certificates on completion. It was written because Moodle felt wrong, and the whole design argument is that creating a course should not be hard.

Live Frappe Framework app AGPL v3 Vue 3 · Frappe UI Zoom integration
01 · What it is

Built because Moodle's forms were too long.

The origin is refreshingly specific. In 2021 Frappe needed an LMS to launch Mon.School for FOSS United. They evaluated Moodle, found the forms unnecessarily long and the interface confusing, and asked the obvious question — why should creating a course be this hard? What started as a purpose-built tool for one school became a product. That history explains both what the app is good at and what it leaves out.

Structured learning is a three-level hierarchy: a course contains chapters, and chapters group lessons. The nesting is not decoration — the chapter is what sets context for the lessons inside it, which is the difference between a course and a playlist. Batches group learners by course and duration, so the same course content can run repeatedly with different cohorts. Within a batch you can create Zoom live classes directly from the app, and learners see the sessions they are expected to attend.

Assessment covers quizzes with single-choice, multiple-choice and open-ended questions, plus assignments that learners submit as PDFs or documents. Certification issues a certificate when a learner completes a course or batch, using a built-in template or one you supply.

The stack is Frappe Framework with a Frappe UI (Vue) frontend. Note one install dependency that catches people: the app expects frappe/payments alongside it, so a local setup that fetches only lms will not come up cleanly.

The name moved, the identifiers did not

The product is now branded Frappe Learning. The repository is still frappe/lms, the bench app is still lms, the container image is still ghcr.io/frappe/lms, and the route is still /lms. The documentation lives at docs.frappe.io/learning. Expect to see both names in the wild and do not assume they are two different products — searching for "Frappe LMS" and "Frappe Learning" returns results about the same app at different points in its life.

02 · Scope

A teaching tool, not a compliance system.

The gap between "we need training" and "we need auditable proof of mandatory training" is where LMS projects go wrong. Frappe Learning sits firmly on one side of it, and being clear about which side saves an expensive discovery later.

Requirement Frappe Learning Notes
Author and deliver coursesYesChapters, lessons, a deliberately light authoring flow.
Cohort-based deliveryYesBatches by course and duration, with Zoom live classes.
Quizzes and assignmentsYesSingle-choice, multiple-choice, open-ended; PDF or document submissions.
CertificatesYesBuilt-in template or your own.
SCORM / xAPI contentNoIf a vendor ships you SCORM packages, this is not the platform for them.
Mandatory-training compliance reportingNot out of the boxNo recurring-assignment engine, expiry tracking or exception dashboards. Buildable on doctypes; not shipped.
Proctoring and exam integrityNoAssessment is designed for learning, not invigilation.
Accredited qualification managementNoNo SETA reporting, credit accumulation or unit-standard mapping.

The compliance question decides the project

"Every employee completes POPIA awareness training annually, and we can prove it to an auditor" is not a course-delivery requirement. It needs recurring assignment, expiry dates, escalation when someone lapses, and a report that survives scrutiny. None of that ships here. It is genuinely buildable — batches, enrolments and completions are doctypes, and Frappe HR is on the same site with the employee records — but it is a build, and it should be scoped and quoted as one rather than assumed into the licence-free column.

03 · How it works

Install, and the dependency people miss.

# Local setup — note the payments app, which is easy to miss
bench new-site learning.test
bench --site learning.test add-to-hosts
bench get-app https://github.com/frappe/payments
bench get-app https://github.com/frappe/lms
bench --site learning.test install-app lms

# App serves at http://learning.test:8000/lms
# Production via the easy-install script
wget https://frappe.io/easy-install.py

python3 ./easy-install.py deploy \
    --project=learning_prod_setup \
    --email=you@example.co.za \
    --image=ghcr.io/frappe/lms \
    --version=stable \
    --app=lms \
    --sitename learn.example.co.za
# Completions are documents, so reporting is a query — not an export
GET /api/resource/LMS Enrollment
  ?filters=[["member","=","learner@example.co.za"]]
  &fields=["course","progress","creation"]

A 404 after deploy is usually DNS, not the app

The install script's own note, and worth repeating because it burns an hour otherwise. On a public server the domain's A record must point at the server's IP. Locally, the domain must be mapped to 127.0.0.1 in /etc/hosts. Frappe is multi-tenant and resolves the site by hostname, so a request arriving under a name it does not recognise gets a 404 that looks like a broken deployment rather than a name-resolution problem.

04 · Gotchas

Where it gets tricky.

Video hosting is your problem

The single largest hidden cost in any LMS project. Course video has to live somewhere and be delivered at watchable quality to every learner. Serving it off the same box that runs the app works for a pilot and falls over for a cohort. Decide early — object storage plus a CDN, or an external video platform embedded into lessons — and cost the egress. This is not a Frappe Learning limitation so much as the part of e-learning nobody puts in the proposal.

Zoom integration means a Zoom account and its constraints

Live classes are scheduled from the app but hosted by Zoom, so you inherit Zoom's licensing, participant limits, session duration caps and recording storage. A department expecting to run 200-person sessions on a basic plan will discover the ceiling live, in front of learners.

The payments app is a hard dependency

Setup requires frappe/payments even if you are not selling courses. A bench that fetches only lms fails in a way that reads as a broken install rather than a missing prerequisite.

Open-ended questions need a marker

Quizzes support open-ended questions and assignments accept PDF or document submissions — both of which require a human to assess them. That is a recurring staffing cost, not a feature you switch on. Automated marking is limited to the choice-based question types.

Certificates prove completion, not competence

Worth saying out loud to a client who is buying "certification". The certificate records that someone finished a course in your system. It is not an accredited qualification, it carries no external recognition, and there is no identity verification behind it. If the requirement is a credential that means something outside the organisation, this is not that.

05 · Decision guide

Use it for, skip it for.

Use Frappe Learning when

  • You are already on Frappe and want learners, employees and courses on one site with one permission model.
  • The teaching model is cohort-based — batches with a start and end, live sessions, a certificate at the end.
  • Course authors are subject-matter experts who will abandon anything with Moodle's form density. Low authoring friction is the product's whole thesis.
  • You are running customer or partner training and want it on your own domain and infrastructure rather than a third-party platform.
  • Onboarding is the use case, and Frappe HR is already on the site holding the employee records.
  • Per-learner SaaS pricing is the blocker and self-hosting removes it.

Skip Frappe Learning when

  • You need auditable mandatory-training compliance — recurring assignment, expiry, exception reporting. That is a build, not a feature.
  • You have SCORM or xAPI content from a vendor. No support; the content is unusable as-is.
  • Accreditation matters — SETA reporting, unit standards, credit accumulation. Nothing in the product speaks that language.
  • Assessment integrity is required. No proctoring, no invigilation, no identity verification.
  • You cannot answer the video-hosting question. Without a plan for delivering video at scale, the project stalls after the first course.
  • You are not otherwise on Frappe. A standalone bench for an LMS alone is hard to justify against mature hosted alternatives.
06 · South African context

Where this lands in SA delivery.

Data cost is the design constraint, and video is the enemy

The most important design decision for an SA rollout is not in the app. If learners are on mobile data, an hour of high-definition video is a meaningful share of a monthly bundle, and the training that costs a learner their airtime is the training that does not get completed. Practical mitigations: keep video short and offer a low-bitrate option, put the substance in text and images that the lesson structure supports natively, use the quiz and assignment types for the assessment weight rather than long-form video, and schedule Zoom sessions knowing that audio-only participation may be someone's rational choice. Design for the bundle, not for the boardroom Wi-Fi.

Skills development, honestly framed

Training carries real weight in South Africa — B-BBEE scorecards, SETA grant recovery, the Skills Development Act. It is tempting to position an internal LMS as contributing to that. Be careful. Accredited training requires accredited providers and registered programmes, and an internally issued certificate from a self-hosted platform is not that. Frappe Learning is an excellent vehicle for genuine internal capability building, product training and partner enablement. It is not a route to points on a scorecard, and anyone implying otherwise in a proposal is setting up a difficult conversation with a verification agency later.

POPIA · learner records are personal information, and results are sensitive

An LMS accumulates more than names and email addresses. Assessment results, completion rates and progress data are performance information about identifiable people, and in an employment context they can feed into decisions with real consequences. Self-hosting answers the residency question cleanly. It does not answer the others: decide who may see individual results as opposed to aggregate reporting, set a retention period for learner records after someone leaves, and tell learners at enrolment what is recorded and who sees it. That last one is a processing-notice obligation, not a courtesy.
