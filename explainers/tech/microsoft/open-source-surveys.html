Replacing SurveyMonkey on a Microsoft stack: mostly yes, with one paid gap.

If your business runs on .NET, SQL Server and Azure, you can own your survey platform — the questions, the answers and the database they live in — instead of renting it per seat in dollars. There is a credible open-source backend for exactly that stack. What there isn't, yet, is a fully free drag-and-drop form designer to go with it. This page compares the real options, says plainly what is free and what isn't, and when Microsoft Forms is the better answer.

.NET · SQL Server · Azure Endatix Survey Solutions Microsoft Forms Designer is paid
01 · The question behind the question

A survey tool is three things, and only two are free.

SurveyMonkey bundles three jobs that open source splits apart. Knowing which part you're replacing tells you what it will cost.

1 · Designer

Where you build the survey

Drag-and-drop questions, logic, branching, themes. This is the part most open-source options on .NET don't give you free.

2 · Runner

What respondents see

The form on a phone or laptop, validation, partial saves. The SurveyJS Form Library does this well, free under MIT.

3 · Backend

Where answers live

Storage, versions, access control, exports, webhooks. Endatix provides this on .NET with SQL Server or Azure SQL, free under MIT.

03 · Endatix, up close

The best Microsoft-stack fit — as long as you plan for the designer.

Endatix is a headless survey backend: a REST API with no screens of its own. It stores forms as SurveyJS JSON and handles what SurveyMonkey does behind the scenes:

  • Form lifecycle: drafts, publishing, versioning (edit a live survey without breaking earlier answers), templates and folders.
  • Respondent features: partial submissions people can resume, prefilled forms, one response per person, password-protected forms, reCAPTCHA.
  • Files: uploads of documents, images, audio and video to Azure Blob storage, Amazon S3 or self-hosted storage.
  • Integration: webhooks on submissions and form changes, CSV and Excel export, email notifications.
  • Access: multi-tenancy with tenant isolation enforced by the data layer, role-based access, and single sign-on over OAuth 2.0 — so Entra ID can plug in.

The paid gap, spelled out

Respondents see forms through the SurveyJS Form Library, which is MIT-licensed and free. But the drag-and-drop designer, SurveyJS Creator, “requires a commercial license for each software developer” in production — licensed per developer, not per survey or respondent. Endatix's own admin app, Endatix Hub, is a commercial Next.js product. Your three routes: buy the Creator licence for your developers, buy Endatix Hub, or have survey authors work in JSON through a simple admin screen you build.

What a Microsoft-native build looks like

Endatix API on Azure App Service or containers · Azure SQL for forms and responses · Azure Blob for uploads · Entra ID for staff sign-in · SurveyJS Form Library in your own site for respondents · webhooks or exports into Power BI or Dataverse for reporting.

04 · Survey Solutions, up close

Built for statistics offices and fieldwork, not marketing surveys.

The World Bank's Survey Solutions is used by national statistics offices, central banks, NGOs and universities. It comes complete: a Designer for questionnaires with sharing and collaboration, a Web Tester, a Headquarters app for managing surveys and collecting both interviewer-led and online responses, and an export service that writes to statistics packages. Android apps let interviewers work offline, with supervisors able to hold and redistribute work where there's no connectivity.

Two things to weigh. The database is PostgreSQL, not SQL Server, so it's “Microsoft stack” in language more than in data platform. And the licence is the World Bank's own community agreement: royalty-free, but read it before building a commercial product on top.

05 · Decision guide

Own the stack only when owning it buys you something.

If you need…Choose
Internal polls, event sign-ups, staff feedback, and you have M365Microsoft Forms. Already paid for; no build.
Client-facing surveys where you must own the data, schema and hosting regionEndatix + SurveyJS, budgeting for the designer licence.
A survey product you sell to your own clients, multi-tenantEndatix — tenant isolation and webhooks are built in.
Household or field research with interviewers, offline tablets and supervisorsSurvey Solutions.
A complete free platform, and the stack isn't a hard ruleLook beyond .NET: LimeSurvey (PHP, GPL) or HeyForm (Node, AGPL) are more complete out of the box.

Self-hosting pays off when

  • Responses are personal information and you need to say exactly where they're stored
  • Surveys feed your own systems through webhooks, not exports by email
  • You run surveys for many clients and per-seat SaaS pricing scales badly
  • You already have .NET developers and Azure hosting

Stay on SaaS or Forms when

  • A handful of people send occasional surveys
  • Nobody will own patching, backups and uptime
  • You need SurveyMonkey's panels or benchmark data — no open-source tool replaces those
  • You'd be the first production user of a pre-1.0 project for a critical workload
06 · South African context

POPIA, rands and offline fieldwork.

POPIA is the strongest argument. Survey answers are often personal information. Self-hosting Endatix on Azure SQL in the South Africa North region puts the data in-country under your own access controls — a cleaner answer to “where is it stored?” than a US-hosted SaaS. With Microsoft Forms, check where your M365 tenant keeps Forms data before you promise anything.

Dollar seats versus a one-off licence. SaaS survey tools bill per seat in USD. A per-developer designer licence and Azure hosting you already run is a different cost shape — usually better once many staff or clients send surveys.

Fieldwork without signal. For research teams, NGOs and municipalities collecting data in areas with patchy coverage, Survey Solutions' offline Android interviewer is the feature that matters most.
