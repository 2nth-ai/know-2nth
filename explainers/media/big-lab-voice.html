Big-lab voice.

The frontier labs stopped treating speech as a separate product and pulled it inside the model. OpenAI ships a realtime family plus standalone TTS and transcription; Google ships Gemini TTS and the Live API with voice replication and a two-speaker mode; Anthropic ships no audio API at all — a fact worth stating plainly, because a great deal of writing implies otherwise. This leaf covers what each one actually exposes, why bundling changes the latency arithmetic, and the cases where a specialist vendor still wins.

01 · What this is

Voice stopped being a separate purchase.

For most of the last decade speech was a vendor you bought next to your model. The frontier labs have been collapsing that into the model itself — unevenly, and not all of them.

There are two architectures here and conflating them is the most common mistake in this area. The pipeline is STT, then an LLM, then TTS: three calls, three bills, three failure modes, and a full text round trip in the middle. The native-audio model takes audio in and emits audio out over one session, with no text bottleneck between hearing and speaking. OpenAI’s realtime family and Google’s Live API are the second kind; their separate TTS and transcription endpoints are components for the first.

Native audio buys two things pipelines cannot easily get: a shorter critical path, because nothing waits for a complete transcript, and information that text throws away — hesitation, emphasis, a caller trailing off. It costs you the thing pipelines are good at: inspecting, logging and testing a text transcript at every stage. Pick deliberately. A regulated contact centre that must evidence what was said often wants the pipeline precisely because it is inspectable.

The strategic read: if you already build on a frontier model, the voice layer is now an option on your existing bill rather than a procurement exercise. That is a real reduction in friction, and it is also how specialists get squeezed — not by being beaten on quality, but by being unnecessary.

02 · OpenAI

The widest surface.

OpenAI covers both architectures, with a realtime family for native audio and conventional endpoints for pipeline builds. Model ids below are from the published API specification.

ModelKindWhat it is for
gpt-realtimeNative audioThe conversational session — audio in, audio out, tools, interruption. Dated pins exist; use one in production rather than the floating id.
gpt-realtime-translateNative audioLive speech translation as a first-class mode rather than a prompt trick.
gpt-realtime-whisperNative audioThe transcription model available inside a realtime session, for when you want the session’s own text stream.
gpt-audioAudio-in chatAudio as an input modality on a normal request/response call — not a live session. Good for “understand this clip”.
gpt-4o-mini-ttsTTSThe current synthesis model. Takes an instructions field — plain-language delivery direction, which tts-1 and tts-1-hd ignore. Input caps at 4096 characters.
gpt-transcribe, gpt-4o-transcribe, gpt-4o-mini-transcribeSTTBatch transcription. gpt-transcribe adds a languages shortlist and a keywords bias list — the two knobs that fix domain vocabulary.
gpt-4o-transcribe-diarizeSTTSpeaker-attributed transcription via the diarized_json response format. Does not accept a prompt.
whisper-1STT (legacy)The hosted open-weights model. Still callable, no longer the lead option — see the STT leaf.

Built-in voices run alloy, ash, ballad, coral, echo, fable, onyx, nova, sage, shimmer, verse, marin and cedar, and you can reference a custom voice by id. Custom voices must be built from audio samples — voices invented from a text prompt are available only in the live surface. Output is mp3, opus, aac, flac, wav or pcm, with speed from 0.25× to 4×.

The detail worth lifting out: instructions on gpt-4o-mini-tts is the same idea as Hume’s acting directions and the reason the older tts-1 models now feel inert. If you inherited a codebase pinned to tts-1, that is a one-line upgrade with a real quality difference.

03 · Google

Two speech stories under one brand.

Google has Gemini-native audio and the older Cloud Speech line, and they are different products with different endpoints, quotas and regional stories. Knowing which one someone means saves a wasted sprint.

Gemini TTS

Synthesis through the Gemini API on gemini-3.8-flash-tts. You set response_modalities: ['AUDIO'] and a speech config naming a prebuilt voice (Aoede and friends) or a replicated one. A language_code steers synthesis.

The Live API

Google’s native-audio session — the equivalent of OpenAI’s realtime surface, with streaming input, tool responses and an audio-transcription config so you can get text alongside the audio.

Chirp, on Vertex

The Cloud Speech-to-Text and Text-to-Speech line, reached through Vertex AI rather than the Gemini API. Separate quotas, separate regional endpoints, and the place to look when data location is a procurement requirement.

Two things Google does that the others do not

A two-speaker dialogue mode. The multi-speaker config takes a list of speaker-to-voice mappings and requires exactly two speaker configurations — the names must match the speaker labels in your prompt. For two-host podcast segments, dialogue practice and role-play scripts this is one call instead of a stitching pipeline. Three speakers is not a configuration option, so plan around it rather than discovering it late.

Voice replication as a config object. A replicated voice sits in the same voice-config slot as a prebuilt one, so cloning is a parameter change, not a different product. Convenient, and exactly the kind of convenience that gets a consent step skipped — see section 05.

04 · Anthropic

There is no Claude voice API.

This needs saying flatly, because a lot of market commentary lists Anthropic alongside OpenAI and Google on voice, and the architecture consequence is significant.

Claude has a voice mode in Anthropic’s own apps. The API is a different matter: its published surface covers messages, batches, models, files, skills and organisation administration, and contains no audio, speech or voice endpoint of any kind. There is no Anthropic TTS, no Anthropic STT, and no Anthropic realtime audio session to call.

So “a voice agent on Claude” always means Claude doing the reasoning inside somebody else’s speech layer. That is a well-trodden path, not a limitation — Hume’s EVI lists Claude models among its bring-your-own-LLM options, and every orchestration platform in the voice agents leaf will put Claude in the think step between a third-party STT and TTS.

What it does mean is that the convenience argument for big-lab voice — one vendor, one bill, one latency path — does not apply to Anthropic. If that convenience is what you are buying, your choice is OpenAI or Google. If you want Claude’s reasoning, budget for a pipeline and choose its speech components on their own merits.

How to answer the client question

“Can we do this with Claude?” — yes, with a speech layer around it, and here is what that layer costs and who processes the audio. Not “Claude does voice.” The distinction matters when the data-processing map goes to an information officer, because the audio processor is a different company from the reasoning processor.

05 · The trade-offs

What bundling actually costs.

Native audio is genuinely better for conversation and genuinely worse for several things teams discover after they have shipped.

Bundling wins on…

The conversational path

  • Latency — one session, no text bottleneck, no three-vendor round trip
  • Paralinguistic information — hesitation and emphasis survive, where a transcript discards them
  • Interruption handling — the session owns turn-taking rather than you stitching it
  • Procurement — one vendor, one contract, one invoice, one data-processing agreement
  • Tool use — the same tool definitions you already wrote work inside the voice session
Bundling costs you…

Control and inspectability

  • Component choice — you take the lab’s voice quality and accent coverage as given
  • The audit trail — no clean text boundary to log, redact and test at
  • Portability — a native-audio session is a deeper dependency than three swappable calls
  • Cost visibility — audio tokens are harder to forecast than characters and minutes
  • Data residency — the realtime surfaces are the least regionally flexible part of either platform
  • Telephony fit — neither is as 8 kHz-native as a vendor built for phone lines

Cloning is a parameter now. Consent is not.

On both platforms a replicated or custom voice is a field in a config object, which makes cloning a staff member or a client trivially easy to do and trivially easy to do without authority. Under POPIA a voice is personal information and a clone is personal information about an identifiable person; the responsibility sits with whoever commissions it. Keep a written, specific consent artefact with every voice you create, and do not let “it is just a config value” stand in for a decision.

06 · Decision guide

Big lab or specialist.

The honest default is: if you are already on a frontier model and voice is a feature of your product, start with the lab. If voice is the product, price a specialist against it.

Use big-lab voice if…

Voice is a feature

  • You already build on OpenAI or Gemini and want fewer vendors, not more
  • You need live translation — OpenAI exposes it as a mode rather than a build
  • You want audio understanding, not just synthesis — “what is in this recording”
  • You want two-speaker dialogue in one call — that is Google’s
  • Procurement overhead is a real cost for this client
  • You are prototyping and want a voice path working this afternoon
Choose a specialist if…

Voice is the product

  • Telephony is the channel and you need 8 kHz-native output — Cartesia
  • You need emotion as a readable signal — Hume
  • You need breadth — dubbing, cloning, music, sound design on one invoice — ElevenLabs
  • You need best-in-class transcription with keyterm biasing and diarisation — see the STT leaf
  • You need self-hosting or in-country inference; no frontier lab offers it
  • You need a pinned voice that will not drift across a multi-year contract
  • You want Claude’s reasoning — then there is no bundled option and the pipeline is the design
07 · South African context

What lands in SA.

Data residency is where this gets decided, and the answer is probably no. There is a Johannesburg region, africa-south1, and Vertex AI lists it — but it lists it for the ML-platform features (training, prediction, Model Registry, Pipelines, Feature Store), and generative-AI locations are documented on a separate page. Compute Engine also shows no GPU machine types in those zones, which is a strong hint about what can be served there. Treat “Google has an SA region, so we can run Gemini voice in-country” as unproven until you have checked the generative-AI locations page for the exact endpoint you intend to call. The OpenAI realtime surface is the least regionally flexible part of that platform. If a client requires that call audio does not leave South Africa, establish that at design time, because the honest answer today is that no frontier native-audio surface meets it.

Cross-border transfer. Where audio does leave, section 72 of POPIA governs it and you need a lawful route — consent, or a contractual or adequacy basis. A voice session sends raw audio of an identifiable person abroad, which is a more sensitive payload than the text your product already sends. Treat it as a separate decision with its own record.

Accent coverage. Both labs handle South African English, neither has tuned for it to the bar they hit for US English, and neither covers South Africa’s Bantu languages at production quality. OpenAI’s keywords bias list on gpt-transcribe is the most practical fix available for local proper nouns — street names, suburbs, medical-aid schemes, surnames — and is worth using properly rather than treating as optional.

FX and forecasting. Audio tokens are harder to forecast than per-character TTS, and they are USD-denominated. For a rand-funded client that is a variable cost on an unhedged currency. Model a worst-case month, not an average one, and state the assumption in the proposal.
