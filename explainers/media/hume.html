Hume AI.

The vendor that made emotional expression a field in the payload. EVI scores a caller’s voice across 48 named dimensions and passes them to whichever LLM you plug in; Octave synthesises speech from plain-language acting directions rather than markup. This leaf covers what the API actually exposes, why those 48 numbers are inferences and not measurements, the POPIA question that emotion data raises before anything else, and the cases where empathy is a liability rather than a feature.

01 · What it is

Two products, one bet.

Hume’s bet is that how something was said is structured data you should be able to act on, in both directions — read off the caller, and controlled on the way out.

Everyone else in this band treats prosody as a quality problem: make the output sound less robotic. Hume treats it as an interface. Its conversational product, the Empathic Voice Interface (EVI, now on its third generation), returns a scored vector alongside the transcript — 48 named dimensions, each a float — so your prompt can see that the caller sounds distressed or sarcastic and not only what words they used. Its speech product, Octave, inverts the same idea: instead of SSML tags you write a short natural-language direction and the model acts it.

The structural choice that matters commercially is that EVI is bring-your-own-LLM. Hume supplies the ears, the voice and the turn-taking; the reasoning is a model you select. The published provider list spans Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Groq, Fireworks, Amazon Bedrock, Perplexity, SambaNova, Cerebras and xAI, plus a custom-language-model hook and Hume’s own small ellm. So EVI is a voice layer, not a vertically integrated assistant — you are not re-platforming your reasoning to adopt it.

Worth knowing if you read about Hume a year ago: the standalone Expression Measurement API that once sat alongside these — face, burst and language models you could call on arbitrary media — is gone from the current SDK. It was moved behind a legacy extra and that extra was removed outright. If a plan of yours depended on batch-scoring recorded media, check that it still exists before you design around it.

02 · The product lines

EVI and Octave.

Two namespaces in one SDK. Most teams want one of them, and the one they want is usually not the one they came for.

EVI — conversational

A websocket voice session with turn-taking handled for you. Configs are versioned server-side; tools are declared as JSON specs, including built-in tools and a web-search tool; webhooks fire on chat start, chat end and tool call. The assistant’s own prosody comes back too, so you can log how your agent sounded, not just what it said.

Octave — speech

Text-to-speech built around utterances. Each utterance carries text, an optional voice, a speed multiplier, trailing silence, and a description that is either acting directions or — if you name no voice — a prompt that invents one. Versions 1 and 2 are both callable; version 2 requires a named voice and adds timestamps.

Where they overlap

Both draw on the same Voice Library, with HUME_AI stock voices and CUSTOM_VOICE entries of your own. Octave output is mp3, wav or raw pcm, default 48 kHz — note that it is not an 8 kHz telephony-native API the way Cartesia is, so a phone deployment resamples somewhere.

Octave’s two modes, and the one that bites

The description field changes meaning depending on whether you named a voice. With a voice it is delivery direction — Hume’s own guidance is to keep it under about 100 characters. Without a voice it is a voice-design prompt and the model invents a speaker, which means the same request can return a different person tomorrow.

For anything customer-facing, create the voice once, pin it by id, and treat description as direction only. instant_mode — the low-latency streaming path — enforces this anyway: it requires a named voice and a single generation.

03 · The 48 dimensions

What the numbers are, and what they are not.

The vector is real, documented and genuinely useful. It is also a model’s inference about a stranger’s inner state from an audio signal, and that distinction is the whole engineering problem.

The scored set is published and fixed at 48 named dimensions. They are finer-grained than the usual sentiment buckets — alongside anger, joy and sadness sit awkwardness, concentration, contemplation, craving, empathic pain, entrancement, realisation, nostalgia, and surprise split into positive and negative. That granularity is what makes the signal worth having: “confusion rising across three turns” is actionable in a way that “negative sentiment” is not.

What the numbers are not is ground truth about what someone feels. They are scores from a model trained on labelled vocal expression, and vocal expression is culturally and individually variable. A South African English speaker’s neutral register, an older caller’s slower pacing, a second-language speaker’s careful enunciation, a bad GSM line — each can move these numbers without anything changing in the person. There is no published evidence that the scoring was validated on South African speech, and you should assume it was not.

The design rule that follows: use the vector to adapt, never to decide. Softening tone when distress scores high is a good use. Routing to a human when confusion persists is a good use. Pricing, declining, escalating, flagging for fraud, or scoring an employee on inferred emotion is not — you would be making a consequential decision about a person from a number the vendor describes as an inference.

Emotion data under POPIA

An inferred emotional state attached to an identifiable caller is personal information under POPIA, and it is information the data subject never knowingly provided. Section 9 requires that you collect only what is adequate and relevant to a defined purpose; section 71 restricts decisions about someone taken solely by automated means where those decisions have legal or substantial consequences. An emotion vector retained indefinitely “for analytics” fails the first test. An automated action triggered by it walks into the second.

Practically: name emotion inference in your processing notice, give it a short retention period separate from the transcript, keep it out of any automated adverse decision, and record the decision not to use it that way. Hume’s own usage policy also sets limits here — read it before you design, not after.

04 · Architecture

Bring your own model.

EVI’s LLM is a configuration value, which makes it unusually easy to adopt and unusually easy to misconfigure.

You point a config at a provider and a model id and EVI drives the conversation around it. The practical consequences are worth stating plainly.

Good: your reasoning, prompts and tool definitions stay portable. If you already run Claude or GPT behind a product, EVI is a voice front-end rather than a migration. Swapping the reasoning model is a config change, not a rewrite.

Less good: the model list is long and includes plenty of older ids. Choosing one off a dropdown without checking what it is gets you a conversation whose latency and quality you then blame on the voice layer. Pick the model deliberately, and prefer the newest one you have actually tested.

Watch the data path. Audio goes to Hume, the transcript and emotion vector then go to your chosen LLM provider, and tool calls go to your own services. That is two or three processors in the chain and possibly two or three jurisdictions. For a POPIA-scoped deployment, each one needs an operator agreement, and the chain needs to appear in your processing record. Choosing a US-hosted LLM behind a US-hosted voice layer is a decision about cross-border transfer whether or not anyone wrote it down.

05 · Pricing reality

Two meters, not one.

We are not quoting figures — they move faster than this leaf’s review cadence, and a stale number is worse than none. The structure is the part worth internalising.

An EVI call bills on Hume’s own meter (voice session time) and on your LLM provider’s meter (tokens), and those are separate invoices. A long call with a chatty, expensive reasoning model costs considerably more than the voice-session figure suggests, and the second invoice is the one that surprises people.

Before committing, price one representative call end to end: voice-session minutes, LLM input and output tokens including the system prompt on every turn, any tool-call round trips, and telephony minutes if there is a phone line involved. Then multiply by your real call volume rather than your pilot’s. For a rand-funded client, both meters are USD-denominated, so FX belongs in the proposal as a stated assumption with a review trigger.

06 · Decision guide

When to use Hume — and when to skip.

Hume is the right answer to a narrow question. If emotional nuance is not load-bearing in your product, you are paying complexity for a field you will not read.

Use Hume if…

Delivery is the product

  • The conversation is emotionally loaded — wellbeing, bereavement, debt, complaints — and tone changes the outcome
  • You want acting direction in plain language rather than hand-tuned SSML
  • You want to keep your own LLM and add a voice layer around it
  • You need a signal for escalation — sustained confusion or distress routing to a human
  • You want your agent’s own prosody logged for review, not just its words
  • You are doing character or narrative work where one voice must play several registers
Skip Hume if…

Empathy is not the job

  • The task is transactional — balance checks, OTPs, appointment slots. Emotion is noise there
  • Latency is the hard constraint — Cartesia is built for that and Octave is not 8 kHz-native
  • You need African-language coverage; nothing in this band delivers it, Hume included
  • You want one vendor for everything — dubbing, music, sound design. That is ElevenLabs
  • You would be tempted to act automatically on the emotion score — the compliance exposure is real and the signal will not carry the weight
  • You need on-premises or in-country inference — this is a hosted API with no self-host path
  • Your stakeholders cannot articulate which decision the vector changes. If nobody can, you are buying a demo
07 · South African context

What lands in SA.

The validation gap is the headline. South Africa has eleven official spoken languages and a very wide range of English registers. A model trained predominantly on North American and European vocal expression will read some of those registers wrong, and there is no published SA validation to appeal to. Before any pilot goes live, run your own check: record consenting speakers across the accents and home languages your callers actually have, score them in a neutral state, and look at where the vector lands. If neutral SA English speakers score as agitated, you have found out cheaply instead of in production.

Language coverage. Both EVI and Octave are strong on English and thin to absent on South Africa’s Bantu languages — the same gap the landscape leaf names across the whole band. Hume is not the exception.

Three processors, one processing record. Audio to Hume, transcript plus emotion vector to your LLM provider, tool calls to your own stack. Under POPIA each external leg is an operator relationship needing a written agreement and a lawful basis for cross-border transfer. Map it once, early. It is much harder to retrofit after a client’s information officer asks.

Where it genuinely earns its place. Debt collection, medical-aid support, bereavement and claims calls, student support — conversations where a caller is already stressed and tone is not cosmetic. Used to adapt delivery and to hand over to a person sooner, this is the most defensible version of the product. Used to score or sort people, it is the least.
